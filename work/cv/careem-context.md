# Careem: context from Art (for picking CV content, not CV text)

Told by Art on 2026-11-24. Background only. Art said not to write the CV
section yet; more context is coming.

## Role and setup

- Hired to **lead the team responsible for the integration with Hala**
  (the Hala team).
- **Careem:** ride-hailing service, owned by Uber when Art joined.
- **Hala:** Dubai's city taxi.
- The integration goes through an intermediary company, **Trapeze**, which
  provides the hardware and software of the **taximeter** that the Careem
  app connects to.
- The taximeter is approved by the **RTA**, Dubai's Roads and Transport
  Authority (Art said "Road Traffic Authority").
- The team built features that depend on the Trapeze integration and on
  alignment with the RTA and Hala.

## The real challenge: alignment, not engineering

- Hard not in engineering terms but in the amount of alignment needed to
  approve even the simplest change.
- Example: tens of hours of meetings to align the protocol on a single
  boolean change in the protobuf.

## Not for the CV (Art's private context)

## Role in practice (part 2)

- Acted as the **engineering lead**, though the contract said Senior
  Engineer II. Made most of the team's engineering decisions: all the mobile
  ones and some backend ones. He worked only on mobile, not on backend code.
- The team was **distributed**.

## What he worked on

- Several Trapeze-related features; Art does not remember them in detail
  and thinks most are already in the LinkedIn text.
- **HDL (High Demand Locations):** the big feature. Art designed it and led
  the main communication around it. He does not know whether it is still
  live or was finished.
- Maintained **all the docs** related to Trapeze.
- Created a **remote testing setup with the meter**.
- Updated the **protobuf protocol** between the meter and the mobile app.
- Most of his time went into alignment with Trapeze.
- Not his role (the squad had a couple of engineering managers), but he
  **proposed Scrum procedures** to the squad. They were adopted to some
  degree and helped; no numbers to prove it.
