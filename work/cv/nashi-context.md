# Nashi.Global: context from Art (for picking CV content, not CV text)

Told by Art on 2026-11-24. Background so the CV can pick the right
highlights; most of this does not go in the CV.

## Team and idea

- Art and his wife moved to Latin America. She is a senior product manager
  and designer.
- They partnered with a local blogger who writes about immigration and has
  connections among owners of immigration Telegram channels and in the
  Russian-speaking immigrant community.
- The three of them built a **marketplace, mostly for services that expats
  provide to other expats**, focused on the Russian-speaking community. The
  whole app is in Russian.

## Growth plan

- Use many ambassadors: they post about Nashi.Global on their channels, and
  in return their channels or services are listed in the app. The hope was
  that it would go viral. It worked to some degree.

## Product and tech

- Looked good, thanks to a highly skilled designer on the team (Art's wife,
  as understood).
- Art wrote everything: frontend in **Vue.js**, backend in **Python/Django**.
- Not very sophisticated, but not a standard web app either: it is a web app
  **inside Telegram**, with deep Telegram integration. It opens from the
  browser and as a **Telegram mini-app**, and has **bot workflows** for
  moderation, communication with users, and more.
- About **2 months from idea to launch** (Art corrected from 3).
- From LinkedIn (not repeated by Art today): launched in 3 countries; an AI
  agent system that monitors Telegram chats and finds leads through an
  AI-based funnel.

## What happened

- It ran for a few months. The partner, who owned marketing and all
  communication with channel owners, planned to move and had no time.
- It worked at first, then hit a wall. Art's assumption was that expats are
  a paying audience, ready to pay for good, familiar services from
  compatriots. Finding people who wanted to advertise for free was easy,
  but users did not pay enough to get traction.
- They tested different product hypotheses and approaches; traction stayed
  hard. When the partner left, the project was left afloat.
- Now about to be closed. Art: the startup was built on a wrong assumption
  about willingness to pay.

## What Art took from it

- A great learning opportunity: technically, in business, in choosing
  partners, and in product decisions across monetization, user acquisition
  and more, not only on the technical side.
